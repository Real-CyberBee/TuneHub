FROM FINGER TO AIR

The three parts of a note

01 / CONTACT

A rounded touch

A short, smooth contact pulse starts the motion. Its duration and strike position change the mix of overtones.

The pulse lasts roughly 1–8 ms, depending on the playing gesture.
02 / PLATE

The metal keeps moving

Parallel resonators ring near the note's 1×, 2× and 3× frequencies. Each has its own damping, so the tone changes as it fades.

Slightly shifted partner modes create slow beating.
03 / SPACE

A little room around it

Some notes gently wake nearby resonances. A short generated room response adds air after the metal's own ring.

The long, pitched tail comes mainly from the resonators, not the room effect.

AND THE MELODY?

Variation with memory

The generator remembers recent notes and develops short motives. It varies density, touch and breathing across a phrase, then chooses the next note within the instrument's note layout.

This is a lightweight modal approximation, not a recording or a full simulation of a steel shell. Real handpans have more complex interactions than the model can capture.
